01Positioning and scope

LossPacket should feel like a carefully prepared case file: clear sources, visible questions, named reviewers and a precise next action. Its website should sell the value of document preparation while keeping insurance decisions with the appropriate professionals.

Audience: a US small-business owner preparing a commercial-property or business-interruption claim, with a broker or licensed adjuster supervising within their permitted role. Brokers are also prospective buyers of tracking; accountants and restoration firms are potential referral or evidence contributors.

Stage: research and proposed product previews. No operating product, customer adoption, revenue, proven time savings, integrations, accepted pricing or payout impact has been established. LossPacket is the user-selected name; domain availability and trademark clearance have not been established.

This is an information architecture and execution specification, not website or app implementation. It includes a deterministic clickable-demo script, not a built clickable prototype. Desktop/mobile captures are specified for the next design stage; none are represented here as completed screenshots.

The company brief takes precedence over the master guide. Retain five separately testable offers, one initial pilot module and the explicit research-stage disclosures. Adapt the guide’s results page to evaluation methodology, its integrations page to a factual data-input note, and its authentication pattern to an honest access-status page. Do not manufacture a customer wall, checkout, platform availability or company history to fill a template.

Recommended first pilot: offer 2, the incident timeline and evidence organizer, as the bounded entry point. Its pilot packet may include a broker-reviewed working paper and coverage-question references as supporting artifacts; this does not turn all five offers into a mandatory commercial bundle. Validate each offer separately before deciding on packaging.

03Distinctive design direction

Use a quiet editorial direction with the working title The Evidence File. The signature visual is a source-linked loss packet, with thin document rules, numbered exhibits and a restrained evidence margin. Specific information should provide visual interest: a disputed date, a missing source, a reviewer note.

DirectionComposition and characterDecision
The Evidence FileWarm paper, ink, plum accents; generous editorial headings beside a dense document workbench. Exhibit tabs and source lines form the visual language.Recommended: combines human care and professional seriousness without looking like a carrier portal.
The Review DeskWhite and dark olive; structured work surfaces, compact typography, reviewer initials and approval margins. More operational and broker-oriented.Alternative if brokers become the dominant buyer.
The Recovery LedgerIvory, charcoal and copper; timeline-led storytelling, ledger typography and financial-paper detail. More owner-oriented.Alternative; take care that financial emphasis does not imply recovery promises.

Proposed identity: a custom LossPacket wordmark and a compact folded-document mark with an offset source tab. Avoid shields, checkmark seals, dollar signs and upward-growth arrows. Produce original SVG primary/reverse/monochrome lockups, favicon at 16/32px, touch icon and social preview before high-fidelity design. Logo assets are a next-stage deliverable, not supplied by this IA.

Starting design tokens: paper #F7F5EF, surface #FFFFFF, ink #242526, secondary text #555A5C, plum #543D58, border #DAD8D0. Use separate labeled amber “Held,” red “Needs attention” and green “Reviewed” states; green must never imply insurer approval. Validate all final color combinations for contrast. Suggested typography: Newsreader for selected editorial headings and Source Sans 3 for body/UI; tabular figures for amounts. These are design proposals, not purchased or installed assets.

Layout: 1200px content container, 680px prose width, 24px mobile gutters, a 4/8/12/16/24/32/48/64/96px spacing scale, modest 8px control radii, 44px minimum intended touch targets. Use three different section compositions: editorial split, full-width workbench and numbered offer rows. Avoid endless identical card grids, oversized gradient headlines, floating dashboards and generic AI illustration.

Motion: source selection highlights the corresponding row; a reviewer action updates a visible state and dated log. Use short opacity/position transitions, no autonomous “approved” animation. Native scroll remains intact. Reduced-motion mode shows all information immediately. Hover may reveal a border or move a small arrow; it must not expose otherwise inaccessible information.

07Homepage and pricing FAQ copy

QuestionProposed buyer-facing answer
Is LossPacket available today?LossPacket is a research-stage concept. You can explore a fictional demo and express interest in a supervised pilot.
Will it tell me what my policy covers?The proposed workspace organizes policy references and questions for professional review. It does not determine coverage or promise a payout.
Who reviews the packet?A broker or appropriately licensed professional within their lawful role would supervise a pilot. The required role depends on the activities and jurisdiction.
Can I upload my claim documents here?No. The public demo uses synthetic records. Any real pilot would require separate written consent, agreed handling arrangements and a redacted sample.
Does it connect to my books or carrier?No live integrations have been established. The concept shows source-linked bookkeeping records and insurer requests using fictional files.
Will it send or file anything?The concept demo cannot send responses, file claims or appeals, determine coverage, move funds or write to financial systems.
What will a pilot cost?We are testing $750–$2,500 for bounded case preparation and a separate $150/month broker-tracking concept. These are hypothetical test prices, not active plans or accepted market pricing.
Do I have to buy all five modules?No. The five offers are separate research directions. A first pilot would select one bounded job; packaging would be decided only after buyer feedback.
Does joining the waitlist authorize use of my claim?No. Updates, a contact request and permission to handle claim materials are separate purposes.
How do I remove my information?The final privacy notice will provide the verified deletion route before live lead collection begins. The demo must not ask you for actual claim records.
How will you measure value?Through reviewer-confirmed evidence gaps, preparation effort and a real paid-pilot decision. We will not treat demo clicks or claim outcomes as proof of preparation value.

08Buyer journeys and conversion logic

  1. Owner arriving from a broker: Home qualifier → Claims Review → missing-evidence demo → limits → price hypothesis → pilot intake. Preserve offer 2 and source attribution; no document collection at this stage.
  2. Broker or licensed adjuster evaluating fit: professional solution → workspace roles and limits → demo override → evaluation method → broker-tracking hypothesis → scoped pilot discussion.
  3. Accountant or restoration referrer: contributor solution → allowed evidence contribution → referral/conflict review explanation → contact. No referral commission signup or automatic partner status.
  4. Curious but unqualified visitor: ungated demo → resources → optional updates. Do not classify demo clicks as qualified demand.
  5. Existing claim needing an immediate decision: visibly direct coverage and claim-rights questions to the visitor’s appropriate professional. Do not imply that a research waitlist can satisfy a claim deadline.

State design for every form: empty → valid/invalid → submitting → confirmed receipt or recoverable error. Preserve ordinary input after an error; never display received/scheduled without an actual successful delivery. Prelaunch design uses “Preview only — nothing submitted.” No scheduling promise or response-time SLA until staffed.

14Pilot plan and acceptance or kill criteria

Demand hypothesis: an owner will pay for evidence preparation independently of the eventual claim outcome, and a supervising professional will find the packet useful enough to consider another referral. Episodic acquisition, referral economics and licensing may constrain the model. A large business is a hypothesis, not a conclusion from vendor adoption.

  1. Review the proposed activities and referral arrangement for the relevant jurisdiction with qualified counsel/professional input. If an unavailable public-adjuster license is required, stop that activity.
  2. Obtain written consent, a bounded paid-pilot agreement and a supervising professional with an appropriate lawful role. Select one offer; record the price actually discussed and accepted.
  3. Use one real redacted property/BI packet through an agreed handling process. Establish the professional’s document checklist and definitions of missing, incomplete, irrelevant and disputed evidence before evaluation.
  4. Compare the starting packet with the reviewed packet using that checklist. Track owner preparation time consistently, separating active preparation from waiting, professional work and correction effort.
  5. Compare with prior comparable cases only where permissioned records and meaningful comparability exist. Record claim complexity, source availability and recall limitations. One case cannot establish causal time savings or payout effects.
  6. Record errors, omissions and false-positive missing-item suggestions; ask whether preparation was valuable independent of settlement. Track later referral intent separately from actual repeat referral.
DecisionProposed gate
Go to one bounded pilotLicensing/referral review passed; consent and data handling agreed; professional appointed; signed paid scope; selected offer recorded. A polished demo cannot satisfy these gates.
Pilot acceptanceReviewer confirms every selected-scope item has a traceable source or explicit unresolved label; no undisclosed critical omission; owner and reviewer can inspect/override assumptions; actual preparation effort recorded; buyer confirms preparation value independent of outcome.
Evaluate improvementAgree any quantitative target before starting. Report missing-item count and severity before/after, corrections and elapsed/active time with denominators. Do not invent a universal reduction threshold from one synthetic example.
ReviseProfessionals need extensive cleanup; owners do not understand proposed pricing; record inputs are inadequate; preliminary channel cost exceeds plausible case margin. Re-scope and retest rather than claiming validation.
Kill or haltUnavailable required license; no lawful consent/access; unsafe data handling; material unresolved professional boundary; repeated critical omissions; payment demand only contingent on payout; or no independent willingness to pay after a pre-agreed capped test.
ExpandOnly after several permissioned evaluations, viable channel economics, actual repeat referrals and policy/claims expertise plus licensing review for any additional insurance line.

Proposed research cap: 10 qualified interviews and up to 3 scoped paid-pilot proposals over 4 weeks, then review. This is a suggested experiment, not a market benchmark or a mandate to run it now. Do not equate 0/3 commitments with a universal market conclusion; document objections and whether the test reached the correct buyer.
